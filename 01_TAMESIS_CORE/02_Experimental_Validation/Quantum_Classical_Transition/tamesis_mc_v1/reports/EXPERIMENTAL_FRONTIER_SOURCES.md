# Experimental frontier sources for Tamesis M_c v1

This file records the external sources used to populate `literature_points.csv`
and to evaluate whether the current data are close to a Bohr-level test.

## Observed center-of-mass matter-wave records

1. Eibenberger et al., "Matter-wave interference of particles selected from a
   molecular library with masses exceeding 10,000 amu", PCCP 2013.
   Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/1310.8343

2. Fein et al., "Quantum superposition of molecules beyond 25 kDa", Nature
   Physics 2019.
   Source: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41567-019-0663-9

3. Pedalino et al., "Probing quantum mechanics with nanoparticle matter-wave
   interferometry", Nature 2026.
   Source: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-09917-9

## Planned or proposed routes toward the threshold

5. Kaltenbaek et al., "Testing the foundation of quantum physics in space via

## Excluded from Tamesis v1 likelihood

9. Bild et al., "Schrodinger cat states of a 16-microgram mechanical oscillator",
   Science 2023.
   Source: https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.adf7553

This is excluded because it is an internal phononic/mechanical mode with a very
small physical displacement, not a wide center-of-mass matter-wave interference
test. It remains scientifically important, but it is not the correct endpoint
for the sharp Tamesis M_c prediction.

## Current conclusion

The best observed center-of-mass matter-wave point is still far below Tamesis
`M_c`. The best proposed target found so far is the 2026 nanodiamond/chip
proposal, because its mass range crosses the Tamesis threshold.

That proposal is not evidence yet. It is a potential experimental road to the
decisive region.
