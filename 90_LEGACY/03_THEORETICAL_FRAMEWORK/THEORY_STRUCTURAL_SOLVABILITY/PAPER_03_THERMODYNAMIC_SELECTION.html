Abstract: In Class B (Universal) problems, internal geometric constraints are insufficient to force a unique solution (The Density Obstruction). We propose that the resolution of such problems requires a perspective shift from Internal Deduction to External Selection. We demonstrate that Thermodynamic Stability (Entropy Maximization) acts as a rigorous selection principle, identifying the "physically realizable" configuration from the infinite sea of mathematical possibilities.

We have established:

  1. Paper 1: Class B problems (Riemann, P vs NP) are structurally distinct from Class A (Poincaré).

Definition 1.1 (Structural Selection)

A selection principle $\mathcal{S}$ is a functional on the solution space $\Omega$ that assigns a "Stability Cost" to each configuration. The "Physical Solution" is the configuration that minimizes this cost globally (Phase Stability).
